The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction

Bloquito

What is art? What art is reality? Who gives it value?

What is art? What art is reality? Who gives it value?

These are the three main questions that I take away from this reading. The author Benjamin is the technical creation of art and the way it is weaponized as a form of expression under facism and the opposite effect under communism. “The growing proletarianization of modern man and the increasing formation of masses are two aspects of the same process.” (p. 19). When he speaks of the masses being engaged, entertained or educated I think about Banksy and the film “Exit Through the Gift Shop” where they investigate “authenticity of art” and its value. Banksy works with stencil art in a modern time, recognized more as a street art style. At the same time, like Andy Warhol, his art is expensive and lives in museums even if that was not his intention. 

Benjamin gives the analogy of a painter as a magician. The magician is farther away from their subject in order to conjure a form of reality, an interpretation of it. “The painter maintains in his work a natural distance from reality,” so is Banksy also a painter? I think he is and I think the method of stenciling also allows him to interrupt reality by where the stencil is placed. Most of the time I see stencils in public spaces commenting on a social matter or an injustice. I believe stenciling is aligned with protesting and campaigning art that is made by any means necessary. Not just art for arts sake.

At the same time Banksy’s work is popularized by its replica. When it is replicated there is mass distribution and it does become an art of the people. In “Exit Through the Gift Shop” the aura of the art is interrupted because it is now replicated as a mass commodity and the authorship is plagiarized as if it were to be an authentic original copy. Benjamin speaks to the idea of authentic original print no longer being available once photography is introduced. Similar to stencil art, the original can easily be replicated. However, when it is part of popular culture the commodification does not need to be lucrative. 

In my case, I also had a Banksy stencil created to have in my room when I studied abroad in my early 20’s. I just wanted something I enjoyed to look at every day that was affordable. My piso mate created and project a large panda with a pistol on my painted yellow wall that she made for me. I never made any money from this and my landlord actually appreciated it and kept it when I moved out. For me, the access to this art at the time was a way to connect with a larger community that I was away from and also connecting with. So I believe it is important to also make art that is for the working class because they are also inspiring the reality of art.

In Defense of the Poor Image

The idea of focus in Woody Allen’s film stood out to me as an example of what the poor image represents. Value placement held by a selected few in cinema who determine what good or high quality is versus poor quality. "Focus is identified as a class position, a position of ease and privilege, while being out of focus lowers one’s values as an image." The character in the film is an actor but because his face appears as low resolution he isn’t desired for many roles.

Steyerl says "the contemporary hierarchy of images, however, is not only based on sharpness, but also and primarily on resolution." The end goal for the quality of cinema is to mimic 35 mm film. She says, “Poor images are poor because they are not assigned value within the class society images…” Juan Garcia Espinosa believes these poor images will lead to a collective like consciousness pulling from third cinema where the line between viewer and author are blurred. Copies and various versions of the original make up remixed and new media that consumers can share in a digital age.

This makes me think of the early days of YouTube and how content creators were using consumer phones and cameras to shoot and create stunts or short viral videos like “La Caida de Edgar” where a little kid gets a bridge pulled from under him by his friend letting him fall in the stream below. In the youtube link I found the title says "(the original)" which lets me know there have been multiple versions of this little guy falling in the water. The author also wrote in the description “download the best quality of this video 39.5mb” of this video with the link. Even users of what would be considered a “poor image” are still aiming for the quality class standard of a good image. Yet, these images are the ones that are creating a digital cult culture. This video has been around now for about two decades and continues to be a cultural reference, unfortunately, of a chunky little kid falling as a prank.
